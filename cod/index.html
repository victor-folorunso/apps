COD Concept project

Buy together, pay less.

Group buying for big-ticket items in Nigeria: solar systems, inverters, batteries, air conditioners. Buyers join a two-week batch for free, the price drops as more people join, and everyone pays the seller on delivery. The app never touches the money.

Recorded from the app: batches, a batch's price steps, filters, installers, and switching to the seller side.

How it works

  • Each batch has a price ladder: every step needs more buyers and unlocks a lower price. Everyone pays the lowest price the batch reaches.
  • Joining is free. You can leave until 48 hours before the batch closes; if it falls short, nobody pays anything.
  • Payment is cash or transfer to the seller on delivery, once the item arrives as described. Problems can be reported from the order.
  • Optional installation by a vetted local installer, paid to them once it works.
  • One account, three sides: buyer, seller or installer. A switch on the Account tab shows only the side in use.
  • Sellers can link their store, but only on Jumia, Konga or Amazon, so buyers are never sent to an unknown website. The database enforces the same list.

Built with

React Native (Expo SDK 58) on Android and iPhone, with Firebase for sign-in and data. There is no server code: the database's security rules are the server, allowing only the moves the app's rules allow (join, leave, ship, confirm), and a live test checks them.

Status

COD was built and tested in 2026 and parked before release. A group-buying app lives or dies on filling batches in one city at a time, which is a marketing problem more than a software one.
